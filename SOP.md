# Piano Score Refiner — Refinement Rules and Acceptance Criteria

[English](SOP.md) | [简体中文](SOP.zh-CN.md)

This document accompanies [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) and defines data parsing, notation decisions, and acceptance checks for piano score refinement. See [README.md](README.md) for the project overview and reference toolchain.

## 1. Input Formats and Available Data

Different input formats contain different kinds of information. Establish what each file provides before processing it:

| Input | Available information | Missing information or items requiring additional checks |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw performance MIDI** | Pitches, start/end times, per-note velocities, tempo, and channel controller events such as sustain pedal CC64 | Reliable meter, measure alignment, separate hand tracks, clefs, enharmonic spelling, slurs, and fingerings |
| **MusicXML (`.musicxml/.mxl`)** | Measures, meter, voices, clefs, pitch spelling, notated durations, ties, and expression markings | Original unquantized microtiming; some exports may omit individual velocities or pedal events |
| **MuseScore project (`.mscz/.mscx`)** | Editable score structure, voices, layout properties, playback parameters, and sound configuration | Opening on another device depends on locally installed soundfonts |
| **PDF / score screenshot** | Page layout, visual symbol positions, ledger lines, and voice appearance | Structured note objects and playback timing; scanned material needs OMR or manual transcription before editing |
| **Original audio** | Audible performance, rhythmic feel, texture, and the presence of other instruments | Reverb tails alone cannot reliably distinguish finger-held duration from pedal sustain |

Distinguish three kinds of position during processing:

1. **Absolute time:** Start/end times in seconds produced by the transcription model.
2. **Notated position:** Measure and beat after tempo/beat mapping.
3. **Layout coordinates:** Positions of noteheads, stems, and page breaks generated by the notation software.

### Transkun Transcription Output

For Transkun 2.0.1 (`pretrained/2.0.pt`):

- Model events contain `start`, `end`, `pitch`, and `velocity`.
- `ModelTransformer.targetMIDIPitch` includes piano pitches `21–108` and the negative channels `-64` and `-67`. `writeMidi` writes positive values as Note events and converts negative values into the corresponding MIDI CC events: `CC64` for sustain and `CC67` for soft pedal, excluding `CC66`.
- “No pedal extension” in the upstream documentation means that training-label note ends are not uniformly extended to pedal release. The model still outputs separate pedal controller events.
- Upstream implementations: [Transkun](https://github.com/Yujia-Yan/Transkun), [Data.py](https://github.com/Yujia-Yan/Transkun/blob/main/transkun/Data.py), and [ModelTransformer.py](https://github.com/Yujia-Yan/Transkun/blob/main/transkun/ModelTransformer.py).

## 2. Establishing the Baseline and Event Table

1. **Save the baseline:** Record the file source, processing range, and operating mode. Back up the original MIDI and current score project before editing.
2. **Parse structured data:** Read the project, XML, or MIDI directly. Check instrument transposition and octave-shift semantics (`8va` / `octave-shift`) before interpreting pitches, avoiding double transposition.
3. **Build an event table:** Assign a stable index to each note and retain before/after data:
   - Core properties: `pitch_midi`, calculated pitch name, `start / end` with units specified as ticks, seconds, or beats, `velocity`, and `track / channel`.
   - Global and controller events: tempo map, meter, and controller events including time, channel, CC number, and value.
   - Notation properties: `measure`, `beat`, `staff`, `voice`, `written_pitch`, and `tie_group`.
4. **Calculate scientific pitch:** Use middle C = `C4` (MIDI `60`) and `octave = pitch // 12 - 1`. Reference values: `60 = C4`, `64 = E4`, `72 = C5`, `84 = C6`, `91 = G6`, and `98 = D7`.
5. **Expand the timeline:** Fully interpret voice cursors (`backup / forward`), dots, tuplets, pickups, and meter changes. Treat same-pitch notes connected by ties as a single attack event.

## 3. Quantization and Polyphonic Notation

- **Separate timing from notation:** Distinguish original performance microtiming, the beat-quantization grid, notated durations, pedal sustain, and reverb decay.
- **Tempo and meter:** Use original audio or an existing tempo track to determine meter and rhythm. Do not force rubato into a single fixed BPM. Disclose algorithmically inferred meter or quantization grids.
- **Record quantization:** When first quantizing raw MIDI into notation, record how snapping affects short notes, ornaments, and merged attacks, and retain the original performance MIDI.
- **Make beats readable:** Rest spelling and beaming should reflect the meter. For example, check half-measure boundaries in `4/4` and group `6/8` around two dotted-quarter beats. Keep measure counts and durations consistent across staves, filling empty measures with rests.

### 3.1 Voices, Chords, and Redundant Rests

For notation-only refinement of an existing project, do not requantize. Reconstruct note, tie, and rest intervals (`start` to `end`) by staff and voice:

- **Chord-merging conditions:** Notes may share a voice and stem only when they start simultaneously, have the same notated duration, and have matching tie relationships. Notes that begin together but have different durations, such as a dotted quarter and a quarter, must remain in separate voices. Do not shorten long notes, lengthen short notes, or average velocities to make them share a stem.
- **Voice allocation:** Keep voice numbering stable along musical phrases and remove meaningless fragmentation into extra voices. Before merging voices, check the entire time interval for conflicts or broken ties across measures.
- **When to hide a redundant rest:** Hide an auxiliary rest only when it fills an internal voice gap, its full interval (`start` to `end`) is covered by another visible note or tie on the same staff, and hiding it leaves beat positions readable. Keep rests visible at independent phrase pauses or where omission would make the rhythm ambiguous.
- **Preserve whole-hand silence:** When an entire hand/staff is silent for a beat or a full measure, retain clear visible rests. Never hide all rests solely by voice number.
- **Preserve internal duration structure:** Change only the visibility property, such as `visible="no"` or MuseScore's `visible` property. Retain internal `Rest` objects and voice cursors so measures stay complete and MusicXML import does not trigger `Incomplete measure` errors.
- **Distinguish editing and printed views:**
  - In MuseScore's editing view, hidden elements are shown in light gray by default and omitted from exported PDFs. Black rests remain visible score elements.
  - To hide those gray marks in the editor as well, turn off “Show invisible” in the View menu and save `Score/showInvisible = 0` in the `.mscx` project. This property is saved per project; see MuseScore's [writer.cpp](https://github.com/musescore/MuseScore/blob/main/src/engraving/rw/write/writer.cpp) and [read460.cpp](https://github.com/musescore/MuseScore/blob/main/src/engraving/rw/read460/read460.cpp).

## 4. Hand Allocation and Fingering Suggestions

- **Allocate by musical texture:** Identify melody, bass line, inner voices, and arpeggiated accompaniment first. A pitch threshold is only a starting point. The right hand may also play nearby accompaniment while carrying the melody.
- **Calculate held notes and spans:** At every new attack time `t`, count notes still held by each hand using `start <= t < end`, including ties across measures but excluding sound sustained only by pedal. Assess maximum simultaneous span, simultaneous note count, surrounding leaps, and hand crossings.
- **Verify hand transfers:** Before transferring a note, consider the receiving hand's existing held notes and movement into the next beat. For example, if the right hand plays `C6/G6/D7` while the left holds `E4`, moving `C6` to the left creates an excessive `E4–C6` span and is not a viable transfer.
- **Handle difficult spans:** Use an octave as a conventional hand-span reference by default. If a wider span cannot be resolved through hand allocation without changing notes, retain the notes and report the measure. Let the performer decide whether to roll the chord, omit notes, shift octaves, or use another arrangement.
- **Add fingerings selectively:** Add a small number of useful editorial fingering suggestions at hand-position shifts, large leaps, or important voice transitions. Identify them as suggested fingerings in the accompanying notes.

## 5. Score Readability and Reading Aids

- **Clefs and octave lines (8va):** Prefer standard treble and bass clefs. For excessive high-register ledger lines, consider a local dashed `8va` marking with an explicit range. Check whether it also shifts other voices on the staff, and verify that sounding playback pitches remain unchanged.
- **Pitch-name labels:**
  - When the user needs help reading dense ledger lines or leaps across octaves, add labels such as `C2`, `E1`, or `G6`, using middle C = `C4` and the original accidental spelling.
  - Label first occurrences or easily misread positions sparsely; do not repeat labels throughout repeated patterns or tied continuations.
  - Use ordinary text elements with no playback effect rather than the Fingering field. Position labels away from slurs, stems, and neighboring notes.
- **End-of-system clef reminders:** If a small clef at the end of a system duplicates the clef at the start of the next system and the user wants a cleaner page, hide only the repeated display. Preserve the actual clef-change event and its time, and render the score to confirm the next system's starting clef remains visible.
- **Dynamics and expression:** Adding dynamics such as `p` or `mf`, or pedal markings, can alter playback velocity or controller output. Check individual velocities and controller events after adding them.

## 6. Protecting Playback and Controller Data

- **Per-note velocity:** Explicitly retain every note's original `velocity` when rebuilding voices or moving notes. Do not average simultaneous chord velocities or reset them to a default constant.
- **Pedal and controller events:** Count and preserve controller events such as `CC64` (sustain), `CC66`, and `CC67` (soft pedal). If an intermediate conversion script loses pedal data present in the original MIDI, repair the conversion chain to restore it.
- **Tempo and sound settings:** Check the tempo map, channels, program numbers, volume, pan, and reverb settings.
- **Two MIDI representations:** If quantization for clear notation cannot preserve performance microtiming, retain both `performance.mid` for original timing and `score.mid` for quantized notation, explaining their purposes.

## 7. Acceptance Checklist

Complete the following checks before delivery:

1. **Event consistency:** In notation-only mode, compare note counts, pitches, onsets, ends, individual velocities, tempo, and controller events before and after editing. Voice or track assignments may change, but the combined musical events must fully correspond within the chosen tolerance.
2. **Structural integrity:** Check meter, pickups, voice durations, ties across measures, and total duration across the grand staff. There must be no double transposition or `Incomplete measure` errors.
3. **Visual rendering:** Render every modified page as an image or PDF and inspect each page for missing notes, overlapping text, colliding lines, incorrect octave-line ranges, or broken pagination.
4. **Software round-trip:** Reopen the generated project in the target software, such as MuseScore, and test export. Confirm that the project structure is valid and view settings, such as hidden-element display, match expectations.

## 8. Delivery Conventions

- Use a stable piece title for final deliverables: the editable project (`.mscz`), printable `.pdf`, and requested `.mid` files. Place intermediate versions and pre-edit baselines in backups. For excerpts, state the measure range in the filename or accompanying notes.
- Include a concise change report describing completed edits, unchanged performance data, inferred meter or quantization settings, and wide-span measures left for the performer to decide.
